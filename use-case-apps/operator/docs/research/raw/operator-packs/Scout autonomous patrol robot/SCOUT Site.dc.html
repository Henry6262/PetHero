THE ECONOMICS ARE BACKWARDS

A $500 drone shouldn't cost a $3M missile to stop.

Modern threats are cheap, autonomous, and everywhere. The systems built to counter them are none of those things. SCOUT puts the economics back on the defender's side — a swarm of low-cost sensor nodes that see first, so expensive effectors are only ever the last resort.

The threat
Commercial drones, jammers, and incursions cost a few hundred dollars and need no operator on site.
The status quo
Fixed radars and missile effectors cost millions, can't enter a building, and don't scale to every perimeter.
SCOUT
A €200 quadruped that walks the perimeter, classifies on-device, and reports before anything gets close.
ONE ROBOT · THREE MISSIONS

Same robot.
Swap the payload.

The platform stays identical. The payload and the operator's ruleset change with the task.

ReconCAMERA ONLY
Patrols a route and flags what changed between passes — a new object, an open door, a missing asset. On-device change detection, no payload risk.
ClearingARM → CONFIRM
Enters structures ahead of personnel, identifies armed threats, and holds a positive ID. No effector fires without a two-step operator confirmation.
RescueNON-LETHAL
Searches disaster zones and contested interiors for civilians, marks them for extraction, and relays a two-way voice channel through the node.

OFF-THE-SHELF, ON PURPOSE

Commodity parts.
Defense-grade behavior.

Every component is commercially available and field-replaceable. That's what makes a €200 price and a defense-grade ruggedized variant the same software, two chassis.
