# Battlefield Environment Elements

**Date:** 2026-07-03

## What makes a map feel like a village battlefield

### 1. Compounds

A compound is a cluster of buildings surrounded by a wall. In tactical maps, compounds create natural "objectives" and chokepoints.

Examples:
- Command post compound (HQ, comms tower, barracks, garage).
- Residential compound (houses around a courtyard).
- Industrial compound (warehouse, workshop, fuel storage).

### 2. Walls

Walls do three jobs:
- Define compounds.
- Block sightlines.
- Create alleys and lanes.

Types:
- **Concrete/cinder-block walls** (urban, Middle Eastern/European village).
- **Adobe/mud walls** (rural, desert).
- **Hesco/sandbag walls** (military forward positions).
- **Wooden fences** (rural European).

### 3. Vehicles

Vehicles make a village feel lived-in and provide cover.

Types:
- Civilian cars, vans, pickup trucks.
- Military trucks, APCs, Humvees.
- Wrecked/burned-out cars.

### 4. Obstacles

Obstacles break up open ground and create cover.

Types:
- Sandbag walls and Hesco barriers.
- Concrete jersey barriers.
- Metal barricades / checkpoints.
- Crates, pallets, barrels.
- Tire stacks.
- Roadblocks.

### 5. Debris and details

- Rubble piles near damaged buildings.
- Potholes and damaged road sections.
- Street lamps, power poles, antennas.
- Drones / camera posts.

### 6. Vegetation

- Trees and bushes in courtyards and edges.
- Avoid placing on streets unless it's overgrown/abandoned.

## Visual hierarchy for Operator

1. **Buildings** — primary structures, status-coded.
2. **Walls / compounds** — frame the village and define sectors.
3. **Vehicles** — mid-size cover, visual interest.
4. **Obstacles** — small cover, tactical detail.
5. **Rocks / vegetation** — natural ground detail.
